Who We Are

Where adventure meets professional growth

Become a travel nurse and expand your clinical skills while enjoying communities rich in culture and breathtaking scenery. Since 1997, our travel nurses have supported diverse Indigenous communities, including First Nations, Inuit, and Métis, in remote areas of Canada, providing care to all ages.

Join Bayshore Northern Staffing for unlimited professional and personal growth, with numerous location choices that cater to your sense of adventure and career aspirations. Our fly-in, fly-out model means your travel is fully covered, so all you bring is your expertise and heart.

Serving northern communities since 1997
Discover Your Journey

Diverse opportunities across Canada's North

Build your career as a travel nurse with meaningful assignments across Canada's North. Choose your own journey, whether short-term contracts or extended placements, across northern healthcare facilities, rural clinics, and remote Indigenous communities.

From Ontario and Manitoba to Alberta, British Columbia, Yukon, Northwest Territories and Nunavut, each assignment offers professional growth while making a lasting impact on the communities you serve.

The Experience

Clinical work unlike anywhere else in Canada

The Experience

Autonomous practice at its finest

Northern travel nursing stretches your clinical skills and broadens your scope of practice. You'll triage, assess, treat, and advocate, often as the primary care provider in the community. It's challenging, deeply rewarding, and career-defining.

"Working up north taught me things about nursing, and about myself, that I carry into every shift now." — RN, Ontario

Food & Culture

Bannock, country food & community feasts

Food & Culture

Taste traditions passed down for generations

The food in northern communities tells the story of the land, from fresh bannock baked in a wood stove to country foods like caribou, Arctic char, and wild berries. You may be welcomed to community feasts, drum dances, and cultural events that leave a lasting mark.

"The community elders invited me to a feast on my first week. I still think about it." — LPN, Manitoba

Real winter temps up north

Embrace the elements

From frozen tundra to the midnight sun, every season in the north is a story worth telling.

The Climate

Cold never felt so alive

Yes, it gets cold, sometimes brutally so. But nurses who've worked up north will tell you the crisp Arctic air, the silence of a snow-covered landscape, and the sheer immensity of the sky are unlike anything else. You'll come home with stories no textbook can teach.

"Stepping outside at −28° under a sky full of stars is something I'll never forget." — RN, Nunavut

Your Residence

A home away from home, fully arranged

Bayshore Northern Staffing arranges all your housing before you arrive. Residences are furnished, heated, and stocked with the essentials. Most are within walking distance of the clinic or health centre, so you spend less time commuting and more time exploring.

Lifestyle & Adventure

Snowshoeing, dog sledding, fishing, and the northern lights, all at your doorstep

Lifestyle & Adventure

Time off like nowhere else on earth

Between shifts, the north opens up. Go ice fishing at dawn. Try snowshoeing through old-growth boreal forest. Join a local hockey game. Catch a dog sled race. In summer, canoe along rivers that have been paddled for thousands of years. The adventure doesn't stop when the clinic closes.

"I saw the northern lights from my backyard every clear night. I'm already planning my next contract." — CHN, Ontario

Natural Wonders

Northern lights, vast skies & untouched wilderness as far as the eye can see

Natural Wonders

The aurora borealis from your backyard

On clear nights, the northern lights dance across skies completely free of light pollution. Nurses often describe this as the single most unforgettable part of a northern assignment, and a reminder of how vast and beautiful this country truly is.

"The first time I saw the aurora, I stood outside for an hour. I cried. It was that overwhelming." — RN, Manitoba

Dramatically different seasons

Every season, a new adventure

Midnight sun in summer. Brilliant autumn colour. Deep winter silence. The return of life in spring.

The Seasons

A year in the north is four worlds in one

Summer means 24-hour sunlight, with evening canoe trips, midnight fishing, and wildflower meadows. Fall blazes with colour before the first frost. Winter is vast, silent, and magical. Spring brings geese, rivers breaking ice, and a community renewed. Each season has nurses coming back for "just one more contract."

Community Connection

Bonds that last a lifetime

Community Connection

You become part of something bigger

In small northern communities, you're not just a nurse. You're a neighbour, a friend, someone people trust. The relationships you build with patients, elders, and community members are unlike anything in a large urban hospital. Many nurses return to the same community year after year.

"The kids in the community used to wave at me every morning. That's why I keep coming back." — RN, First Nations Community, Ontario
